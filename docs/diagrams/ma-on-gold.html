A moving average is not a level on gold

Two things a moving average is meant to do: tell you which way the trend is, and give price somewhere to pull back to. Measured on 5,000 hours of real gold, it does neither — and every pullback tested came back under a coin flip.

It crosses far too often to mean trend

“Price above the moving average is an uptrend” only means something if price stays on one side for a while. On the hour, it does not.

And price does not bounce off it

The test: the moving average is rising, a candle's low reaches down and touches it, and the candle still closes above. The textbook pullback. Then ask what price had done ten hours later.

Not one of the five reached 50%. The 10 and the 20 have the samples to be taken seriously — 700 and 360 touches — and both came back under. Buying gold's pullback to a rising EMA, on the hour, lost more often than it won.

What this settles, and what it does not

  • Settled: an EMA is not a level on gold's 1-hour. Price passes through without noticing. Your drawn weekly and daily lines are levels because price stopped there before; a moving average has no such history.
  • Settled: which side of it is not a trend filter here. Even the slowest tested flips four times a week.
  • Not settled: the slope. This tested touches, not direction. A rising 150 as an “only take longs” gate is a different question and a different picture.
  • Not settled: a slower chart. Everything here is the 1-hour. The daily is a different instrument as far as these numbers go.

This is a hit rate, not a backtest — no stop, no target, a fixed ten-hour exit. A real edge would still show up clearly above half. None of these do.
