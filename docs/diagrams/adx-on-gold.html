Where ADX says there is a trend

ADX does not care which way price is going. It measures whether one side is winning consistently. This is that number drawn on real gold, so the threshold can be settled by eye rather than by the number a textbook prints.

What the number actually counts

For each candle it asks one thing: did this candle reach further up than the last one, or further down? It adds the up-reaches and the down-reaches separately over fourteen candles, then measures how lopsided that split is.

All the reaching one way is a high ADX. Reaching both ways in turn cancels out and gives a low one. It never says up or down — only whether one side is getting its way.

That is also why it is late. Fourteen candles have to happen first, so it confirms a trend that has already been running. It answers “should I be taking trades at all right now”, never “is this the entry”.

The textbook number does not fit gold

Every book says 25. On these 5,000 hours of gold, ADX is above 25 more than half the time — a filter that lets through 57% of everything is barely a filter.

What this does not settle

  • It is one instrument on one timeframe. Gold's normal is not the euro's. Whatever number is chosen has to be checked on the pairs that matter, or it works on the one it was fitted to and quietly stops working elsewhere.
  • It is drawn to make a point. Twenty days chosen because they hold a strong run and a dead patch. That is useful for agreeing what the rule is. It proves nothing about whether the rule makes money — that needs judged signals and a backtest.
  • A high ADX is not a reason to trade. It says a trend exists. It says nothing about where to get in, and by the time it is high the move is old.

Open question: which threshold, and whether ADX earns its place at all next to Choppiness — which answers the opposite question more cleanly.
