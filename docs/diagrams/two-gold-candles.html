One body, one wick

Two days on gold that did opposite things with their range. The 19th spent 87% of itself on body — a decision. The 20th spent 72% of itself on lower wick — a place price went and did not stay.

Real candles. 2 XAU/USD daily candles from Interactive Brokers · 19 and 20 August 2026 · named by nsc-ta against config/candles.toml · "normal" averaged over the 14 days before each one.

Drawn to scale, on one price axis

Same axis for both, so the sizes are comparable. The 19th covers 199.65 points top to bottom; the 20th covers 90.35 — less than half.

The bodies are the filled boxes; the thin lines are the wicks — where price went during the day and did not finish. The 20th's body is nine pixels tall here because it really is that small: it closed 4.89 points from where it opened.

What each day was made of

The same range, split three ways. This is the whole difference between them.

How big is that, against a normal day?

"Normal" is the average true range of the 14 days before each candle — never the candle itself, and never anything after it. On the 19th a normal day was 95.89. On the 20th it was 104.27, because the 19th had just widened it.

What this picture does not say

  • Not a trend. Swings are not wired into the reader yet, so nothing here says which way gold is going. "Long lower wick" is the name the code uses precisely because hammer and hanging man are the same candle and only the trend before it tells them apart.
  • Not a level. The reader knows shapes, not lines. The black zone drawn on the TradingView chart is not in config/pairs/XAUUSD.toml, so the bot cannot see it — a shape only means something once you know what it printed at.
  • Not the 21st. The day after these two is still running and is left out on purpose. Its shape is not its shape yet.
  • Not evidence. This is drawn to make one point clearly. It says nothing about whether a long lower wick at a level is worth trading — that needs judged signals and a backtest.
One thing to check. These numbers are Interactive Brokers'. The same two days on the Pyth feed in TradingView are a few points different, most likely because the daily candle starts at a different hour. Levels read off one feed and judged against the other will not line up exactly.
