Push then pin, on gold and the Aussie

Your two-candle setup — a push, then a pin whose tail points against it. The code swept both pairs across five timeframes and found 19 on each. This page shows what it found and what price did next.

REAL CANDLES. Interactive Brokers, swept 21–22 August 2026 across 30m · 1h · 4h · 1d · 1w, in 6,725 finished candles. Every one was judged against the normal candle size at the moment it printed, never today's. The 38 on gold and the Aussie are below.
This is a measurement, not a backtest. It uses closes only — no spread, no slippage, and no stop anywhere. And none of these had a level under them. A pin bar only means something at a level; that is the part this sweep does not know about.

What the code looks for

Two candles, and the tail decides

Candle 1 — the push. Mostly body, so one side plainly won, and at least the size of a normal candle. Shape alone is not momentum: a quiet candle in a dead hour can be nearly all body and mean nothing.

Candle 2 — the pin. A tail at least twice its own body, pointing against the push, with little or nothing at the other end — and no bigger than the push, because a pullback that covered more ground than the move it answers is not a pullback.

The tail must oppose the push, and that is the whole rule. A tail pointing the same way is the push being refused — a different animal. Ignore which way it points and this fires on twice as much and means half as much.

Read this before acting on the page

Three things this cannot tell you

  • It is not a live read. These are the candles swept on 21–22 August. Nothing here says what gold or the Aussie is doing right now — that needs TWS logged in and --bin read.
  • Trend is not built. Swing highs and lows are found, and trend would be counted off them — but nothing in the code names a direction yet. So no page and no answer here says gold is bullish or bearish.
  • No level was under any of these. That is the missing half. A pin bar in open space is a shape; a pin bar at a level you drew is the setup. This sweep did not know where your levels were.
The sample is under the hundred this project's own backtest rule asks for. The direction of these numbers is clear enough to act on, but the precision is not.

The likely reason for a smaller followed count is that the newest setups had fewer than ten candles after them — but nobody has checked that, so it is a guess and it is written here as one.

It does not change the direction of anything. 29 of 75 is 39%, 30 of 80 is 38%, and both are a long way under a coin flip. It needs settling because an unreconciled count is how a real mistake hides.
