nsc_trades · phase one

Broker to Telegram

Phase one sends signals and places no trades. Forex pairs and gold, and nothing else. Trades are executed on the 4-hour and the 1-hour; the weekly and daily are there for levels. Candles come from the broker finished, and nothing is computed from anything else — the broker's chart is the truth.

One hour, both roads

The clearest way to see it. Here is a single 1-hour gold candle, from the moment it opens to the moment it reaches the strategies.

The whole thing

One connection in, behind one door so a second broker is a config change. Two lanes out.

The price watcher

Sees every tick, including candles still forming.

It watches the levels you drew — which is where your strategies live. So the alert is not a vague "go and look". It means your zone is live: price has arrived where you would be waiting for a setup.

It still may not produce a signal. Arriving at the level is not the setup. The setup is what the candle does there.

The analysis and the strategies

Sees closed candles only. A candle still forming does not exist to it.

The analysis says what is on the chart. The strategies say what to do about it. Keeping them apart is what lets several strategies read one analysis.

Any match becomes a signal, with the sentence explaining it.

What sits inside each stage

The same seven stages, with no arrows — just what each one has to contain before it can be called finished. Nothing here talks to anything else on this page; it is a parts list.

Stage 1

The feed

Getting prices and candles onto your machine, from any broker.

  • One door every broker hides behind — swap the broker, nothing else moves
  • Websocket, one live price per pair
  • One candle request per timeframe close
  • Reconnect on its own when the line drops
  • Logins kept out of the code
  • Four timeframes — W1, D1, H4, H1
  • Forex pairs and gold — nothing else
  • Twelve Data to start — IBKR and OANDA behind the same door later

Stage 2

Keeping it

So a restart costs you nothing and a backtest has something to read.

  • Candles — one row per closed candle, per timeframe
  • Alerts sent
  • Signals sent
  • Setups rejected, and which layer rejected them
  • Outcomes — did it reach the stop or the target
  • Your 👍 and 👎 on each signal
  • A scan that says whether a history is complete

Stage 3

The price watcher

Telling you when price reaches a zone you trade.

  • Your levels, loaded from config
  • The live price for each pair
  • A touch test — is price inside a level's band
  • Fire once per touch, not once per tick
  • Arm again only after price leaves the band
  • Never produces a signal

Stage 4

The analysis

Measuring what is on the chart. Facts only, no opinions.

  • Swings — highs and lows, and the candle that confirmed each
  • Candle types — rejection, engulfing, inside bar
  • Structure — higher highs, lower highs
  • ATR — the size of a normal candle
  • Your levels, and how far price is from each, in ATR
  • Trend on each timeframe
  • The lookahead guard — kills the run if it reads a candle too early
  • Hands over one sheet of facts
  • Draws the chart it just read — candles, your levels in your colours, the swing it found

Stage 5

A strategy

Six questions asked of that sheet. One set per strategy — reversal, breakout, trend.

  • Direction — which way may we trade at all
  • Place — is price where you would take it
  • Trigger — what has to happen before you act
  • Stop — where it goes, in ATR
  • Target — where it aims, and the reward ratio
  • Skip — reasons to bin it anyway
  • The one sentence that explains it
  • Rejections saved, not dropped

Stage 6

When you trade

Whether you get told at all. Applies to every strategy, and to alerts.

  • No Mondays
  • Nothing new on Fridays — open trades still tracked
  • Tuesday to Thursday, normal
  • Not the same setup twice
  • One message when two strategies agree, not two
  • A cap on signals per day — your call
  • Nothing through news — your call
  • A minimum reward ratio — your call

Stage 7

Telegram

The only interface. There is no dashboard.

  • The alert message — your zone is live
  • The signal message — entry, stop, target, reward, why
  • A chart picture with it — what the bot actually saw
  • 👍 and 👎 on each signal
  • A note when a trade reaches its stop or target
  • Says when a check was skipped rather than passing quietly

Stage 8

Proving it

Running the stored history through the same code the bot runs.

  • Replay, one candle at a time
  • The same strategy code as live — never a backtest branch
  • The lookahead guard on, always
  • Marked ambiguous when stop and target were both hit in one candle
  • Win rate, average win, average loss, worst run
  • Which layer rejected the most setups
decided still your call

Nothing arrives on a quiet hour

Silence is the default. There is no routine message, no hourly candle, no chart sent because an hour went by. Send something every hour and by the second week you stop opening them — and then you miss the one that mattered.

WhenWhat arrives
1 Price touches a level you drew An alert. One line, no picture. Gold has reached 4,520. It may fire on a candle still forming — it is only a heads-up
2 A candle closes inside that zone The candlestick. What it actually did there — rejected, closed through, or nothing
3 It closed there and a strategy matched The chart and the candlestick, with entry, stop, target and the sentence
· Morning and evening A heartbeat. Still running, pairs watched, zones touched, signals sent

Rung 2 is the one worth understanding

Price arriving at a level says nothing. It may cut straight through.

The close is what says whether it was a rejection. A candle at 4,520 that closes with a long wick back down is one thing; the same candle closing at its high is the opposite. You only know which once it has finished — which is why rungs 2 and 3 never fire on a candle still forming.

Why a heartbeat at all

Silence has one problem: after three quiet days you cannot tell whether nothing happened or the bot died.

Twice a day rather than once, because twelve hours of silence is believable and twenty-four starts to feel wrong. It doubles as the record that answers why did nothing fire this week?

Two moments, not one

This is the part that was missing. Price arriving at your level and the setup forming there are different events, minutes or hours apart, and they should reach you as different messages.

Price arrivesThe setup forms
What happened Gold traded up into 4520 — your weekly resistance. The 4-hour closed as a rejection off 4520.
Who saw it The price watcher, on a tick. The analysis, on a closed candle.
What it means Your zone is live. Go and watch it. Your rules matched. Here is the trade.
Certainty None. Price at a level is not a setup — it may cut straight through. Full. Every layer of your rules said yes, and the sentence explains why.

The watcher only watches your levels. It is not scanning the whole chart for anything interesting. It knows the handful of prices you drew, and it tells you when one of them is reached — because that is where your strategies live.

Where your rules live

The analysis hands over facts. A strategy is six questions asked of those facts, in order — and a setup has to survive all six. Reversal, breakout and trend are the same six with different answers.

Your strategies are these six, filled in differently

Reversal, breakout and trend each get their own copy of the six. Reversal's place is at a level; breakout's is just beyond one. Same six questions, different answers — and that is all a strategy family is.

All three read the same facts, so the swing reversal sees is the swing breakout sees. Nothing is measured twice. Each lives in its own file in config/strategies/.

When you trade sits outside all of them

Not "is this a good setup" but "may you be told at all". No Mondays. No new trades on Fridays. Tuesday is fine. Plus: not the same setup twice, not fifteen in one morning.

It applies to alerts as well. Keeping it out of the six means changing your Monday rule cannot quietly change what counts as a setup.

Rejected setups get saved, not dropped, along with the name of the layer that rejected them. Those rows answer "why did nothing fire this week?" — and later they are the don't-take-this examples a model would learn from.

What the analysis hands over

The arrow between the two boxes had nothing written on it. This is what travels along it — one sheet of measured facts, the moment a candle closes. Every strategy reads this same sheet.

XAUUSD · 4-hour · closed 15:00 UTC price 4512.40 ATR 18.20 the size of a normal candle your levels weekly 4520.00 0.4 ATR away ← price is in it daily 4602.00 4.9 ATR away daily 4438.00 4.1 ATR away swings high 4521.60 confirmed 15:00 low 4455.20 confirmed 07:00 structure lower highs since 04:00 this candle rejection off the high weekly down daily down

Facts only

Nothing on that sheet is an opinion. Every line is measured, and every distance is in ATR — a multiple of a normal candle — so the same number means the same thing on gold as on EURUSD.

No strategy measures anything itself. It reads the sheet and answers yes or no.

Why it matters that it is one sheet

Reversal, breakout and trend all read the same swing high, the same ATR, the same distance to your level.

If each measured its own, two of them would eventually disagree — and there would be no way to say which was right.

From step 5 the signal also carries a picture of the chart the bot read — the candles, your levels in your colours, and the swing it found. It is our drawing of the broker's candles, not a photo of your platform, so the spacing and styling will not match exactly. If a price differs from your chart that is a real problem worth chasing. If the look differs, that is just us.

The alert has no entry, no stop, no target — because there is no trade yet. The signal has all three, plus one sentence you could argue with. If that sentence cannot be written, the rules are too loose, and that is a rules problem rather than a wording one.

Three things I have been calling "rules"

This is where the confusion came from, and it is mine. They are three different things and they live in three different places.

The nameWhat it isLives in
A strategy A named way of trading. Reversal, breakout, trend. Three of them, and you can add more. config/strategies/reversal.toml
The six layers What a strategy is made of — direction, place, trigger, stop, target, skip. Every strategy has all six, answered differently. This is not a separate thing from the strategy; it is the strategy's insides. the same file
When you trade Applies to everything, whatever the strategy. No Mondays. Nothing new on Fridays. Not the same setup twice. config/app.toml

If you meant a fourth kind, say so. Things like a maximum risk per trade, a minimum reward ratio, no trading through news, or a cap on how many signals in a day are none of the three above — they would sit alongside when you trade as limits that apply to every strategy at once. Worth naming them now rather than discovering them one at a time.

Three things settled, one to go

Price has no timeframe

The watcher does not need to watch four timeframes. Price is one number. Gold at 4312 is 4312 on the weekly, the daily, the 4-hour and the 1-hour at the same moment.

One price stream per pair. It checks each tick against every level you drew, whatever timeframe you drew it on — a weekly line and a 1-hour line are both just a price band to it.

Nothing computes a candle

The websocket sends a price and nothing else — no open, no high, no low, no close. It does not know what a 1-hour candle is.

So candles are asked for, once, a few seconds after each close. The broker built it; we just fetch it. Build one from ticks instead and it would come out slightly different — a different first tick, a high a few cents off — and then your chart and the bot would disagree with nobody able to say which was right.

Rules are not part of the analysis

The analysis
What is on the chart. Swings, candle types, structure, your levels, distances. Facts, measured.
The strategies
What to do about it. Reversal, breakout, trend — each reading the same facts.

That separation is the whole reason several strategies can exist. Mix rules into the analysis and each strategy needs its own copy of the swing finder — and the day two copies disagree, you cannot tell which was right.

The days you trade

Not a setup rule — a rule about whether you are told at all. It sits after every strategy, and it applies to the price watcher's alerts as well as to signals.

Monday
Nothing. No signals, no alerts.
Tuesday to Thursday
Normal.
Friday
No new trades. A trade already open is still tracked to its stop or target.

That Friday rule is the interesting one, because it means the bot has to know the difference between opening something and following something already open — which is a thing it needs anyway for tracking outcomes.

Still open: when is a repeat not news?

A 4-hour reversal that fires at 13:00 is usually still there at 17:00, and at 21:00. Without a rule that is one setup and three identical messages, and by the second week you stop reading the channel.

Same when two strategies match the same setup — one message listing both, not two messages. Cheap to decide now, painful to retrofit once signals are flowing.

Twelve Data, and the two things to check first

Settled: Twelve Data to start. IBKR and OANDA come later behind the same door, which is exactly why the door exists.

Check one: is the websocket on your plan?

Candles are a plain request and work on any plan. The live price stream is the part that is usually paid-only.

Without it there is no price watcher — no "your zone is live" message at all. Everything else still works. Worth knowing which of the two you are building before you start, rather than finding out in week two.

Check two: where does their day end?

We said the broker's chart is the truth. Twelve Data is not your broker. Its daily candle may not close at 17:00 New York, and its week may run Monday to Friday rather than Sunday evening to Friday evening.

Your hand-drawn levels are safe either way — they are prices, and a price does not care where a candle starts. What would shift is the swings, structure and trend read off the daily and weekly.

Ten minutes settles it: pull one daily gold candle, put it next to the same one on your chart, and see whether the open matches.

The request budget is not a problem. About 31 requests a day per pair. Two pairs is 62, eight pairs is around 250 — comfortably inside even a free daily allowance. The websocket is the only thing that might cost money.

The build order

Each step ends with something you can see working. Nothing starts until the one before it does what it says.

The thin pipe

Connect to Twelve Data. Take the price stream for one pair. Fetch its 1-hour candle when the hour closes and send the four numbers to your Telegram channel. No storage, no analysis, no rules.

Proves
Login, price stream, candle fetch and Telegram delivery. Four things that each eat a week — and they eat it whether they are first or last.
Done when
Gold's 1-hour candle closes and your phone buzzes with its open, high, low and close.
Also gives
The real message shapes, written down from what actually arrives rather than from the documentation.

Every pair, behind one door

All the pairs plus gold. Four timeframes — weekly, daily, 4-hour, 1-hour. And the broker hidden behind one interface, so adding IBKR or OANDA later is a config change rather than a rewrite.

Proves
The provider's real limits — how many price streams at once, and how hard the request limit bites.
Done when
It runs a full trading day without dropping a stream or missing a candle.
Why the door now
Two brokers are already planned. Building the door afterwards means unpicking it out of everything written in between.

Keep it

Postgres. One table of candles. Every closed bar written the moment it arrives.

Proves
A restart does not cost you the week.
Done when
You kill the process, start it again, and the only hole in the table is exactly as long as it was down.

The past

Download history, one call per timeframe. Then scan it: are any candles missing, and does the weekend or gold's nightly break account for them?

Proves
You have something honest to test rules against.
Done when
Two years of gold at all four timeframes, and the scan reports zero unexplained holes.
Known already
Gold stops every weekday from 20:45 to 22:00 UTC — its nightly hour off. Spot forex does not stop at all. Measured, not guessed, and now the only exception left.

Reading the chart

Swings, candle types, structure — and your hand-drawn levels loaded from config. All measured on closed candles only.

Proves
The code sees what you see.
Done when
A chart printout puts lines where you drew them, in your colours — black weekly, blue daily, yellow 4-hour.
And a picture
The bot draws the chart it read — candles, your levels in your colours, the swing it found — and sends it with the message. Not a screenshot of your platform, which would need a browser running and would show their lines rather than yours.
Why it earns its place
A level in the wrong spot, a candle that should not exist, a swing where you see none — you spot all of that in a glance and never in a table of numbers. Every signal becomes a check on the code.
Boundary
Here the analysis gets its own crate, with no network dependency in its manifest. Enforced by the build, not by memory.

The price watcher

The other lane. Every tick checked against every level you drew. Price touches one, you get told.

Proves
The alert path works and stays separate from the signal path.
Done when
Gold touches 4520 and you are told — on a candle that has not closed.

The strategies one at a time

One family first, not three. Direction, place, trigger, stop, target, and the reasons to skip. Then the next one, reading the same analysis.

Proves
A setup can be described in one sentence. If it cannot, the rules are too loose — a rules problem, not a wording problem.
Done when
A 4-hour signal arrives with the sentence that explains it, and does not arrive again four hours later for the same setup.

Proving it

Replay the stored history through the same code the bot runs, one candle at a time, with the lookahead guard on.

Proves
The rules are worth following — and that the backtest describes the bot rather than something better.
Done when
A year of gold replays and the guard stays quiet.
Non-negotiable
Never "if we're backtesting, do this instead". The moment that exists, the backtest tests something the bot does not do — and it makes results look better, not broken.

Crates arrive when they are needed

Twelve empty crates was the last version's mistake. Start with one and split when a second thing needs the same code.

From stepCrateWhy then

5nsc-core · nsc-taThe split that is not optional. Shared types, and chart reading that cannot reach the network, because its manifest lists nothing that can.

Not in phase one

  • Anything but forex pairs and gold. No indices, no oil, no silver or copper. That also removes the 16:00 New York problem, which only ever affected the indices.
  • Placing trades. Signals only. Not a gap waiting to be helpfully filled in.
  • The AI check. It can only lower confidence or block — never rescue a setup the rules rejected. It comes after the rules are worth trusting.
  • The news filter and the trained model. Both need a record of signals that does not exist yet.
  • A web dashboard. Telegram is the interface.

Still open

Does your Twelve Data plan include the websocket?

The candle side is a plain request and works on any plan. The live price stream is the part that is usually paid-only — and the price watcher is nothing without it. Check before step one.

Where does their day end?

Twelve Data is a data provider, not your broker. If its daily candle does not close at 17:00 New York, its daily chart is not your daily chart. One candle, compared by eye, settles it.

How many pairs to start?

Two would prove everything eight would, and cost a lot less to get wrong. Gold plus one major.

When is a repeat not news?

Same setup, next candle. One message or three? Cheap now, painful later.

This is a plan, not a result. Every step here is achievable and none of it is proof that the rules make money. That needs step 8 and a year of history.

The previous version reached 15,000 lines and 286 passing tests before it was cleared, on one wrong assumption made before the data layer was settled: that the big timeframes would be built from a small one.
