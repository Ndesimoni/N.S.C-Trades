How Close Counts

An alert fires when price gets near one of his bands. How near should that be — a fixed number of pips, or a share of the band itself? Measured on his own three pairs, and the answer is the same one the band thickness already gave.

Real data · XAU/USD, GBP/USD, EUR/USD · Twelve Data, 15 Aug 2026

The measure that matters is timesettled

Not how many points. How long price takes to cover them — because the whole job of the alert is to give him enough notice to get to his chart before the reaction starts.

Those columns look reasonable. Turn them into hours of warning and one of them falls apart.

Eight pips is six minutes on gold

Each bar is how much notice the alert gives, in hours of normal movement.

Why 8 pips falls over

A pip on gold is ten cents. Gold moves $13 in a normal hour, so eight pips is six minutes — the alert arrives after the move it was meant to warn about.

A pip on the euro is a hundredth of that in relative terms, so the same eight pips is nearly two hours.

It is the exact thing CLAUDE.md already says: 20 pips is a big move on EURUSD and a small one on GBPJPY. A pip setting works on the pair you tested and quietly stops working on every other one.

Why a share of the band travels

The band is already sized by how much that pair moves — 0.35 of a weekly candle. So a quarter of the band is a quarter of something that already fits the instrument.

Same rule, same answer, everywhere. Nothing to set per pair, nothing to forget when a new pair arrives.

And it is the answer his own hand already gave, twice, on the thickness.

The measurement was wrong the first timeworth keeping

The first run of these numbers said gold moves 0.73 in a normal hour, and made eight pips look like an hour of warning.

It was measured on a Saturday. Most of the last forty hourly candles were weekend — near-zero range, because the market is shut — and averaging them in said the market was calm when it was closed.

Gold actually moves 13.33 an hour, and the answer flipped. That is the weekend-candle problem already written down in PROGRESS.md — still not handled in code, and it has now cost one wrong answer.

This is an argument, not a result. It settles what "close enough" should mean. Whether the alerts are worth acting on needs a month of them and a record of what happened next.

Settled: approach = 0.25 in config/levels.toml, a share of the band's thickness. Turn it up for more notice and more false starts.
