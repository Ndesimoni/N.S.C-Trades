The same rules at four timeframes

One series of 30-minute candles, built up into 1-hour, 4-hour and daily through the same aggregator the bot uses, then read by the same four settings. Every swing and every level below came out of the real code — but the candles are made up, and that matters. Read the note at the bottom before drawing conclusions.

Yes — more swings on the lower timeframes, and not because the rule loosened

The four settings are identical on all four charts. Nothing was tuned per timeframe, because every number is a share of a move rather than a distance — half the run, a third of the run, 0.382 of the run.

What changes is how many legs exist in the same three weeks. The daily chart has 19 candles and a handful of turns; the 30-minute chart has 810 candles and dozens. The rule finds the structure that is actually there at each scale, which is the whole point of measuring in ratios.

Notice the levels too: the daily and 4-hour find a few wide ones, the hourly finds fourteen narrower ones. Same rule — the band is half a normal candle, and a normal candle is smaller on the hourly.

These candles are invented, and one thing about them is a lie

This series was built to retrace about half of every move, at three scales, because that is what a real chart tends to do. It is a fair test of the mechanism and it is not evidence about your markets.

An earlier attempt used a series that trended without ever pulling back properly, and the daily chart produced one swing in six weeks. That is not a bug — the rule needs a pullback of 38% or more, and there were none. But it shows how completely the answer depends on the data.

So the real version of this page needs your gold daily export. Same four panels, same code, real candles — and then the question "does it draw the levels I would draw" finally has an answer.
