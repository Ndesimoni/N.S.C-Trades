What a doji is, as four numbers

A candle is measured before it is named. Four numbers come out, and every one of them is a fact two people would agree on. The name comes after, from a threshold you can change.

Real candles. Every candle shown below is a real XAU/USD 1-hour candle from Twelve Data, drawn to its own proportions · 3,546 live candles measured · weekends and holidays removed.

The four numbers

body

How much of the candle is body. 0.20 here — a fifth. A share of the whole, never a price.

upper

The wick above the body, same way. 0.41.

lower

The wick below. 0.39. The three always add to 1.

reach

The only one not a share. The whole candle divided by ATR — is this big for this pair? Three points is nothing on gold and a week on the euro.

Where the name gets decided

A doji is a candle with almost no body. “Almost” is the whole argument, and it belongs in config/ rather than in code.

doji_body

Move it and nothing recompiles — the same candles get a different answer. That is the reason for the split. Bury the number inside a function called is_doji and changing your mind means changing code.

Two things the numbers already say

  • A doji is not rare. At 0.10, one hourly gold candle in nine qualifies — 394 of 3,546. On its own it is not a signal, it is a shape. What makes it worth a message is where it printed, and that is your zones, not this crate.
  • Two in five are too small to mean anything. Of those 394, only 240 reached half a normal candle or more. The rest are dead-hour candles with tiny bodies because nothing happened. reach is what tells them apart, and it is why a share-of-the-candle number alone is not enough.

This is the model, not a rule. Whether a doji at a zone is worth acting on is a question for nsc-strategy and a backtest — nsc-ta only ever says what a candle is.
