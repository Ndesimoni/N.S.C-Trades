What repeated touches do to a level

The same five touches on the same level. Two schools of thought about what they mean — and they point in opposite directions. Your config assumed the left one. You trade the right one.

Worn down

not how you trade

Every touch uses up the orders sitting there. By the fifth, there is nothing left to defend it, so it gives way. A level that has been tested a lot is one to avoid.

Well defended

your rule

Every touch is more people noticing that price and deciding to defend it. By the fifth, everyone is watching. A level that has been tested a lot is one to pay attention to.

One thing kept separate

A well-defended level that finally does break is a big event — probably a bigger one than a weak level breaking. That is a breakout rule, not a level-strength rule, and it does not contradict anything above.

Still a belief, not a measurement

Both pictures are arguments, not evidence. Once there are 200 signals you have judged, this becomes testable: do setups at heavily tested levels actually work out better? The reasoning is written into strategy.toml so it can be checked rather than quietly assumed.

It lives there, and not in ta.toml, because it is a view about trading rather than about drawing. The chart-reading code counts the touches; the rules decide what a lot of touches is worth.
