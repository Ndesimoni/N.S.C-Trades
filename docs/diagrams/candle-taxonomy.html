Twenty-two names, fewer shapes

Every single-candle type on the list, found in three years of real gold. Several of them are the same candle with two names — and the four numbers cannot tell those apart, because what separates them is not the candle.

Real candles. 4,165 XAU/USD 4-hour candles from Twelve Data, 23 Oct 2023 to 14 Aug 2026 · weekends removed · each one is the clearest example found, drawn with the candles either side · ATR worked out as it goes.

Read this before the pictures

Four pairs on the list are one shape wearing two names. The textbook separates them by what came before, not by the candle itself:

These twoAre the same candleTold apart by
Hammer / Hanging Manidenticala downtrend before it / an uptrend
Shooting Star / Inverted Hammeridenticalan uptrend before it / a downtrend
Paper Umbrellathe shape itselfnothing — it is the hammer shape
Long Bullish / Bullish Belt Holdthe same candle herewhether it opened at its low

So this crate cannot name them, and should not try. It reports the shape. Whether a hammer is a hanging man depends on the trend it printed in — and the trend belongs to nsc-strategy.

Building both means two detectors that fire on the same candle, and a backtest counting one setup twice.

1 · Doji — the body is almost nothing

Five ways to have no body

All five need body ≤ 0.05. What separates them is where the wicks are.

2 · Strong body — the body is nearly everything

Six ways to be all body

A marubozu has no wick at all. An opening marubozu has none at the end it opened; a closing one has none at the end it closed.

3 · Small body — indecision

A body, but not much of one

Bigger than a doji, small enough that neither side finished in charge.

4 & 5 · Rejection — a long wick one way

Price went looking and was refused

The shape is a long wick with a small body at the far end. Which name it gets depends entirely on what came before it.

6 · Belt hold

Opened at one extreme and never went back

A long candle that opened at its low and closed well up, or opened at its high and closed well down. No wick at the open end at all.

What the counts say

  • Spinning tops are everywhere — 408 in three years, one candle in ten. A shape that common cannot mean much on its own.
  • A true marubozu is rare. Four bullish and three bearish in 4,165 candles. Almost every "marubozu" a trader points at is really an opening or closing one, with 87 and 43 found.
  • High-wave found exactly one. Either the thresholds are too tight or gold does not make them on the 4-hour. Left as one rather than loosened until a shape appeared — that would be fitting the rule to the wish.
  • The four-price doji is not in the data at all, because it is one of the candles we throw away. All four are holidays. It has no shape and Shape::of answers nothing for it.
