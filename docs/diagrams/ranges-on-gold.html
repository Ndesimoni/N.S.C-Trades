The range indicator is really a coil indicator

Choppiness is built to say “price is going nowhere”. On gold it does mark the tight patches correctly — but what follows is not more of the same. When it says range, price leaves the box almost every time.

What it counts

Two numbers over fourteen candles. How far price travelled candle by candle — every high-to-low added up. And how far it actually got — the highest high to the lowest low.

Lots of movement, no progress, means the first number is much bigger than the second: choppy. One clean run means the two are nearly equal: trending. It comes out between 0 and 100, and it never says up or down.

What happens next is the opposite of the label

The test: take every candle it calls a range, draw a box around the last fourteen candles' high and low, and ask what price did over the fourteen that followed.

Read the two range rows. On the 4-hour, price stayed inside the box twice in a hundred and travelled more than the whole width of it. That is not a range you can sell the top of. That is a spring.

And the trend rows are the calmer ones. When Choppiness says trending, price stays put more often and covers less ground. Backwards, both ways.

So what is it good for

  • Not for trading a range. By the time it says range, the coil is already tight and the break is close. Selling the top of that box lost on this data.
  • Possibly for expecting a break. A high reading marks compression, and compression on gold resolves. Which way it resolves this does not say — it never says direction.
  • The 4-hour is the cleaner read. 2% versus 18% is a wider gap than the hour's 7% versus 23%, on a similar number of samples.

The weekends nearly ruined this

The raw feed hands back Saturday and Sunday candles. On gold they are almost flat — 1,412 of the 5,000 hourly candles had a range under 0.02% of price.

A flat stretch is maximum choppiness by definition, so the first run of this said price travelled 5,119% of its box. That number is a weekend, not a market. Everything above is measured on trading hours only.

This is a hit rate on one instrument, not a backtest. It shows what the indicator marks. It proves nothing about whether trading it makes money.
