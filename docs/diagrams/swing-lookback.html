This describes a rule the project no longer uses. Counting candles either side of a peak was replaced by the run-and-pullback rule: a peak counts once price gives back about half the run that made it. Kept because it records why the change was made — a lazy rounded top with twenty quiet candles around it passed this test, and a sharp turn with four around it failed. Neither matched what you see. What "3 candles either side" actually means

lookback is the most influential number in the project. Every level, trendline, Fibonacci anchor and trend reading is built from the swings it finds. This is what the current setting of 3 asks of a candle, and what changing it would do.

candles ATR swings levels trendlines structure

The question the code asks every candle

Is this candle's high higher than the highs of the three candles before it and the three after it? Six comparisons. Nothing else.

Same chart, different setting

This is the choice in front of you. One series of candles, two answers, depending on how many neighbours a candle has to beat.

What it costs to wait longer

The second half of the setting, and the half that is easy to miss. You cannot know a candle was a peak until you have seen the candles after it. A bigger lookback means a longer wait.

What is not in this picture

Whether a swing is major or minor. The code has one flat list and treats every swing the same, so the only way it currently has of saying "that one was small" is to not find it at all.

If you separate big swings from little ones when you look at a chart, that is a different change — two lookbacks running side by side rather than one number tuned to a compromise.

A diagram is an argument, not evidence. It shows what the setting does; it says nothing about which number makes money. That needs a backtest over real candles, which this project cannot run yet.
